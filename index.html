Who we are

From correction to connection. From surviving to rebuilding.

Maranatha Reentry serves Metro Detroit men, ages 18 and older, who have experienced incarceration, probation, parole or other forms of corrective control.

Our mission

Restore dignity. Build stability.

We empower men returning to their communities after incarceration with access to safe housing, nutritious food, education, disability services and essential resources that create pathways to purpose, independence and lasting success.

Our purpose

A safe place to stand, and people who stay.

Through structured reentry homes, mentorship, employment pathways and community connections, we help returning citizens build the stability, skills and relationships to become productive members of their communities and break the cycle of recidivism.

Our vision

A nation where every man comes home to solid ground.

We see a nation where every man returning to his community has safe, stable housing and the support to rebuild his life with dignity, purpose and hope, and where his lived experience becomes an asset that mentors youth and strengthens public safety.

One home can change a life. A network of homes can change communities. A national model can change the way America approaches reentry.

Two programs that feed each other

We start with words. Then we give men ground to stand on.

Launches first

Writing Our Way Forward

A free 12-week literacy and guided-writing cohort. Men build reading and writing skills, tell their own stories in their own words, and finish with a personal Independence Plan for the year ahead.

  • Up to 12 men per cohort
  • Meets twice a week
  • Hosted by churches, libraries, courthouses and city halls
  • Write by hand, type, dictate to a peer scribe, or record aloud
  • Nothing a man writes is shared without his written permission

Graduates can return as paid peer scribes and mentors.

Opening as funding allows

The Maranatha Reentry Home

A structured transitional house for 6 men at a time, with a part-time house manager, clear house rules, and a path to a home of their own.

  • Help getting ID, food assistance and Medicaid
  • Transportation and job connections
  • Planning for permanent housing
  • For men who need minimal supervision

The house manager role is designed for a man with lived experience.

Our approach

An honest audit of a life

We ask each man to conduct an honest audit of his lived experience. Like a financial audit, it is not a trial or a punishment. It is a careful, fair accounting.

A story left in the head stays tangled. Once it is on the page or said aloud, a man can examine it, own it, and decide what comes next.

Voluntary, alwaysEach man owns his writingDignity in learningVictim-conscious accountabilityCounselor referrals when needed
  1. What happenedThe world he lived in, told in his own words.
  2. What shaped himThe people, places and systems behind his choices.
  3. What he choseHonest ownership, without letting shame become the whole story.
  4. Who was affectedThe harm to victims, families and the public, kept in view.
  5. What he carries forwardAn Independence Plan for housing, work, education, family and growth.

Why this work is needed

When a man goes to prison, his whole family serves the sentence.

Punishment ends legally but continues socially, through missing housing, work, skills and belonging. The consequences follow families home. Maranatha Reentry exists to close that gap.

Michigan's recidivism rate counts only returns to prison. It does not measure whether a man has a home, a livable income, literacy, family connection and a voice. That is what we measure.
